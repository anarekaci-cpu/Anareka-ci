Culinary heritage

Attiéké — Ivorian Culinary Heritage

Attiéké — a know-how passed down for generations

Step 01 / 07 — Harvest

The root of the work

Cassava is harvested at maturity, root by root, by hand — the starting point of all attiéké know-how.

Step 02 / 07 — Peeling

Beneath the skin

Each root is peeled before processing — a gesture repeated thousands of times, day after day.

Step 03 / 07 — Grinding

From solid to soft

The peeled cassava is ground into a paste, ready to ferment.

Step 04 / 07 — Pressing

The time that makes the flavor

The paste ferments, then is pressed to extract the starch — the step that gives attiéké its character.

Step 05 / 07 — Winnowing

Sifted by hand

The semolina is winnowed and sifted, whatever the weather — rain does not stop the work.

Step 06 / 07 — Steaming

Steamed over a wood fire

Steaming follows tradition, over the fire — a gesture passed down from generation to generation.

Step 07 / 07 — Serving

Ready to be shared

Attiéké is served — the fruit of the work of thousands of Ivorian women and men producers.

Who we are

About ANAREKA-CI

An organization at the heart of Ivorian gastronomy, serving a trade federated since 1996.

The National Association of Attiéké Restaurateurs and Kiosks of Côte d'Ivoire (ANAREKA-CI) brings together cassava producers, restaurateurs, vendors and managers of attiéké kiosks.

On the ground

Standing with our attiéké producers

ANAREKA-CI works every day for women's empowerment and the promotion of quality attiéké, produced in keeping with traditional know-how.

Our mission alongside producers

Our association brings together the dynamic producers of the Dibou and Orbaff regions. Together, we structure the cassava-processing trade to guarantee impeccable hygiene standards and lasting social impact.

  • Quality & Hygiene — Rigorous monitoring in partnership with the INHP of Bingerville to certify our processes.
  • Solidarity — Supporting the financial independence of rural women through modernized grinding and pressing.
  • Heritage — Preserving the traditional production method, from winnowing to steaming, while improving working conditions.
